🔹 Explanations & Analysis
1. Layer-wise Adaptation

    Linear Probe: Only the classifier adapts. Backbone features remain frozen, so performance depends entirely on how well pre-trained features transfer. Early layers remain generic (edges, textures), deeper layers remain ImageNet-specific.

    Last Block Fine-Tuning: The final block adapts to dataset-specific semantics. This improves representation quality for new classes while keeping earlier layers stable.

    Full Fine-Tuning: All layers adapt. Early convolutional filters (edges, colors) shift slightly, but deeper layers change more. Risk: catastrophic forgetting of general features, especially if dataset is small.

    Selective Unfreezing (20%): By unfreezing only layer3 + layer4 (~20% of backbone), we balance adaptation and stability. Early layers remain general, deeper layers specialize. This often yields strong generalization without overfitting.

2. Performance vs % Unfrozen Parameters

    Linear probe → ~0% backbone unfrozen → fastest convergence, lower ceiling.

    Last block → ~10–15% unfrozen → better adaptation, moderate compute.

    Full fine-tune → 100% unfrozen → highest capacity, but risk of instability.

    Selective (20%) → sweet spot: enough adaptation for new dataset, but still robust.

3. Gradient Norm Statistics

    Linear probe: gradients concentrated in classifier only.

    Last block: gradients flow mainly in layer4, small in earlier layers.

    Full fine-tune: gradients spread across all layers, risk of exploding/vanishing norms.

    Selective: gradients concentrated in last two blocks, stable distribution.

4. Convergence Stability

    Linear probe: stable, quick convergence, but plateau early.

    Last block: stable, moderate convergence speed.

    Full fine-tune: slower, sometimes unstable (loss oscillations).

    Selective: stable convergence, often best trade-off between speed and accuracy.

5. Conditions Where Fine-Tuning Reduces Robustness

    Small dataset: full fine-tuning overfits, losing generalization.

    Domain shift: early layers adapt too much, losing universal low-level features.

    High learning rate: amplifies instability across all layers.

Underlying mechanism: gradients in early layers overwrite general filters (edges, textures), reducing robustness to unseen data. Keeping early layers frozen preserves transferable representations.


In practice:
Selective unfreezing (last 20%) often gives the best balance of accuracy, stability, and robustness. Full fine-tuning is only beneficial when you have a large, diverse dataset similar in scale to ImageNet.
