Write-up / Analysis
1. Validation Accuracy under Each Regime

    100% data: Highest accuracy, backbone features fully leveraged.

    20% data: Moderate drop, but still competitive if backbone is strong.

    5% data: Significant drop, especially for high-capacity models prone to overfitting.

2. Relative Performance Drop

    Defined as (Acc100−Acc5)/Acc100.

    A smaller drop indicates stronger data efficiency.

3. Training–Validation Gap

    Larger gaps in low-data regimes → overfitting.

    Smaller gaps → better generalization.

🔹 How Model Capacity & Architecture Influence Low-Data Performance

    High-capacity backbones (ResNet-50, deeper CNNs):

        Strong performance with full data.

        In low-data settings, they overfit quickly because many parameters adapt to noise.

        Training–validation gap widens.

    Smaller or regularized backbones:

        Less prone to overfitting.

        Retain better generalization under limited supervision.

        Lower relative performance drop.

🔹 Which Backbone Shows Stronger Data Efficiency

    Architectures with strong inductive biases (e.g., convolutional hierarchies in ResNet) and pre-trained weights demonstrate better data efficiency.

    ResNet-50 tends to be robust in low-data regimes because early layers capture transferable features (edges, textures).

    However, full fine-tuning of ResNet-50 with only 5% data reduces robustness — selective unfreezing or linear probing is more efficient.

✅ In short:

    Linear probe is most data-efficient (small drop, small gap).

    Full fine-tuning suffers in low-data settings (large drop, unstable convergence).

    Selective unfreezing balances adaptation and generalization, showing stronger robustness when supervision is limited.
